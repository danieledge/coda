Before you start

Three things,
and a backup.

1

A Psion netBook

With the CompactFlash card it starts from. The netBook has no flash memory of its own: its system is the file OS.IMG on that card, copied into memory at every start.

2

A CompactFlash card

256 MB or larger. Coda takes about 74 MB; the rest is room for PsiBoot's backups of the Internal disk, PsiLinux's saved sessions and your own files. A 128 MB card works but fills up. Your netBook's own card will do if it has the room. A spare card is the easy way: your old card stays exactly as it is.

3

A computer with a card reader

Windows, macOS or Linux, to download Coda and unzip it onto the card. Nothing to install.

Back up the Internal disk first.

The first time the netBook starts a different system, its Internal disk (C:) starts afresh. Copy your own files from the Internal disk to the card, or to your computer, before you begin. Once Coda is running, PsiBoot does this for you before every switch.

  • Documents: Word, Sheet, Agenda and Contacts files.
  • Data files of programs you've installed, such as their mail folders.
  • Anything in C:\Documents and your own folders.

Install

Four steps.

About ten minutes, most of it the download and the card copying.

  1. 1

    Download Coda.

    Coda comes as one zip of about 34 MB: everything that goes on the card, ready to unzip. It is free: the Download page lists every available release. It has been tried on a netBook with a serial device that behaves like a modem, and in an emulator, but not yet with a LAN (Ethernet) card.

    Download Coda Beta 1 Free, about 34 MB

    Beta 1The first beta: expect bugs, and back up first.Report issues on GitHub

    If Coda is useful to you, you can buy me a coffee.

  2. 2

    Keep a spare, if you like.

    Switch the netBook off, take out its CompactFlash card and put it in your computer's card reader. In the card's top folder is a file called OS.IMG, about 14 MB: your netBook's system. Copy it to your computer as a spare.

    You don't need it to go back: the zip has Psion's own system in it too.

  3. 3

    Unzip it onto the card.

    Unzip the Coda zip into the card's top folder. Let it replace OS.IMG. OS-STOCK.IMG in the zip is Psion's own system, so you can always go back.

    CompactFlash card
├─ OS.IMG          Coda, the most free memory
├─ OS-STOCK.IMG    Psion's own system
├─ LOADER.MBM      the picture while it starts
├─ System\         Coda's programs and files on the card
├─ Documents\      example files to try
├─ Linux\          Alpine Linux for PsiLinux
├─ LICENCES\       whose work is in Coda, on what terms
├─ Coda\           Diagnostics' logs, ready-made
├─ Backup\         PsiBoot's backups of C:, ready-made
└─ README.txt      the same, in short

    Then eject the card properly before you take it out, so every file is finished writing.

    Don't let your computer repair the card. The netBook leaves old bytes after the end of a folder it makes, which it ignores but a Mac's Disk Utility, fsck or chkdsk may report as damaged or cross-linked entries. Nothing is wrong, and a repair can delete those folders, your backups with them. Just eject the card.

  4. 4

    Start the netBook.

    Put the card back in. The netBook reads its system from the card only when it starts from cold: the reset button starts the system already in memory again. So unplug the mains adapter, take the main battery pack out, wait a few seconds, put it back (leave the small backup battery alone) and switch on.

    You'll see the Coda picture and a progress bar while it loads, then Preparing Coda, once, as it sets itself up. Then you're at the System screen.

    Now copy your Documents and other files of your own back to the Internal disk. Not a whole old Internal disk, and never its System folder: that would put the old system's settings, connections and Remote link over Coda's. From now on PsiBoot backs up the Internal disk and restores it with Coda's settings kept right.

Where you'll be after step 4.

Two systems on one card

Updates

Staying up to date.

When a new Coda comes out, download it from the same place and unzip it over the card, replacing what's there. Your documents and settings on the Internal disk are not touched by the card.

Coda's programs that update themselves, such as PsiMail and PsiWeb, can also be updated from their own Tools menus.

Help

If something isn't right.

The netBook says "No Coda card".
The Coda system is running without its card. Put the card back in. Coda full, which works without the card, is coming soon.
A program says "This program is on the Coda card".
Some programs, such as Calc and Sketch, live on the card in the Coda system. Put the card in and open it again.
I want Psion's system back.
In PsiBoot, File, Switch image, choose Psion's own. Or on your computer, rename OS.IMG to OS-CODA.IMG and OS-STOCK.IMG to OS.IMG, then restart the netBook.
The netBook still starts Psion's system.
It is running the system already in its memory. Unplug the mains adapter, take the main battery pack out for a few seconds, put it back and switch on: the netBook then reads OS.IMG from the card.
The netBook doesn't start from the card.
Put the card in your computer and check OS.IMG is in its top folder, then unzip the Coda zip onto it again. Eject the card before you take it out.
I can't format the Coda card: "In use".
The netBook holds some of a Coda card's files open, so it can't format that card, even after switching to Psion's own system. Take the card out and format it in your computer.
A program says a file is "corrupt", or shows a KERN-EXEC error.
Restart the netBook from PsiBoot (File, Restart) and try again. If it comes back, please open an issue with the version from About, what you did just before, and the log from Coda\Diagnostics\System.log on the card (turn on Control panel, Diagnostics first).
The silkscreen buttons open the wrong programs.
This can happen after you restore an older backup of the Internal disk (C:): the old Shell settings come back and no longer match Coda. In Files, open the Internal disk, then System, Apps, Shell, delete Shell.ini and restart the netBook. The Shell makes a fresh one. It only resets your Extras bar choices and your own button assignments (Control panel, Extras bar). If the buttons are still wrong, please open an issue and say which machine you have.
Something went wrong.
Turn on Control panel, Diagnostics. It saves a log of what happened in the Coda\Diagnostics folder on the card. The logs name the servers and web pages your programs used, but not passwords, keys or messages: look through them, then attach that folder to an issue on GitHub.
My computer says the card has errors.
Folders the netBook made itself (backups in Backup\, for example) can look damaged to a computer's disk check, because the netBook leaves old bytes after a folder's list. The netBook reads them correctly. Don't run First Aid, fsck or chkdsk repairs on the card: a repair can delete those folders.
